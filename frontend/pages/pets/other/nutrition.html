Diet Profiles

Species Diet Classifications

Different fish have evolved specialized digestive tracts. Feeding an herbivore high-protein animal meals or starving a carnivore of protein leads to fatal organ failure.

Carnivorous Fish

Predatory and insectivorous fish requiring high protein (45–55%) and lipids. Their short digestive tracts are designed for rapid nutrient extraction from animal proteins.

  • Examples: Bettas, Angelfish, Discus, Puffers
  • Primary Foods: Bloodworms, daphnia, brine shrimp, blackworms, krill pellets
  • Avoid: High-grain filler flakes with excess terrestrial wheat starch

Herbivorous Fish

Plant, biofilm, and algae grazers equipped with long, winding digestive tracts. They require abundant fiber and plant enzymes to prevent bloat and impaction.

  • Examples: Otocinclus, Bristlenose Plecos, Silver Dollars, Tropheus
  • Primary Foods: Spirulina flakes, kelp wafers, blanched zucchini, cucumber, spinach
  • Avoid: Frequent high-protein meaty treats that cause intestinal dropsy

Omnivorous Fish

Adaptable opportunists requiring a healthy blend of quality proteins (35–40%) and vegetable matter to support active metabolism and natural immune function.

  • Examples: Guppies, Mollies, Platies, Neon Tetras, Zebra Danios
  • Primary Foods: Balanced micro-pellets, spirulina flakes, freeze-dried daphnia
  • Best Practice: Rotate between 3 different food types throughout the week
Aquarium Pantry

Food Formats & Nutritional Variety

A diverse diet guarantees essential vitamins, minerals, and carotenoids for optimal vitality and radiant scale luster.

Flakes & Crisps

Processed at low heat to retain vitamins. Float briefly before sinking, making them ideal for top and mid-water community feeders.

Pellets & Wafers

Dense, nutrient-stable formulas engineered as floating, slow-sinking, or sinking discs for bottom dwellers like catfish and plecos.

Frozen & Live Foods

Bloodworms, mysis shrimp, and daphnia mimic natural hunting triggers, provide pure hydration, and condition fish for breeding.

Fresh Vegetables

Blanched zucchini, deshelled green peas, and spinach provide vital dietary roughage that gently cleanses livebearer and goldfish digestive tracts.

Portion Control

Feeding Schedules & The Golden Rules

In nature, fish are opportunistic foragers whose stomachs are no larger than their eye. Master portion discipline to maintain pristine water.

The 2-Minute Rule

Offer only what your fish can consume completely within 90 to 120 seconds. Any uneaten food falling to the substrate must be siphoned out promptly to prevent ammonia spikes.

Frequency by Life Stage

Adult community fish thrive on 1 small meal daily or 2 micro-feedings. Growing juveniles need 2–3 tiny feedings, while tiny fry require frequent infusoria or baby brine shrimp.

The Weekly Fast Day

Instituting one fast day per week allows the fish's digestive tract to clear completely, virtually eliminating bloat, swim bladder pressure, and chronic constipation.

Water Chemistry Hazard

Dangers of Overfeeding

Overfeeding is the number one cause of aquarium fish mortality worldwide. Understand the catastrophic chain reaction it triggers.

Ammonia & Nitrite Spikes

Decaying organic food releases massive loads of toxic ammonia (NH3). Even 0.25 ppm ammonia burns delicate gill filaments, suffocates fish, and crashes biological filters.

Bacterial Blooms & Low Oxygen

Excess organic waste fuels explosive bacterial blooms that turn tank water milky cloudy. Heterotrophic bacteria consume immense dissolved oxygen, choking fish overnight.
