AVIAN NUTRITION BASICS

Nutrition Needs by Bird Species

Every companion bird species has distinct dietary physiology evolved from its natural ecological habitat, from arid grassland granivores to tropical canopy hookbills.

Budgies & Parakeets

Nomadic granivores requiring 50%–60% micro-pellets, daily green chop, controlled millet treats, and essential dietary iodine to prevent goiter.

View Species Guide →

Cockatiels & Conures

Prone to seed addiction and Vitamin A deficiency; thrive on 60% small pellets, orange veggies (squash, carrots), and cuttlebone calcium.

View Species Guide →

Lovebirds & Small Hookbills

High energetic metabolic rates; benefit from 60%–70% pellets, shredded zucchini, sweet corn, sprouted pulses, and foraging puzzle toys.

View Species Guide →

African Greys & Amazons

African Greys require high bioavailable calcium and UV lighting; Amazons require lean vegetable chop to prevent hepatic lipidosis.

View Species Guide →

Macaws & Large Parrots

Naturally adapted to tropical canopy nuts; require higher healthy lipid rations including raw walnuts, macadamias, and palm fruit oils.

View Species Guide →

Lories & Softbills

Specialized nectarivores requiring formulated commercial nectar and pollen; Eclectus require high-fiber fresh produce with minimal fortification.

SEEDS & GRAINS

Seeds & Grains Feeding Guidelines

While an all-seed diet is harmful, controlled portions of quality seeds, wholesome cooked grains, and sprouted pulses provide vital foraging enrichment.

Controlled Portions

Restrict dry seeds to 5%–10% for parrots and up to 15%–20% for active budgies to safeguard against obesity and hepatic lipidosis.

Sprouted Living Seeds

Avian Veterinary Guidance & Wellness Notice

PetSathi's bird nutrition guide is developed for educational awareness. Avian dietary needs vary significantly based on species, health history, age, and flight activity. Always consult a board-certified avian veterinarian before making major alterations to your companion's diet.

AVIAN TOXICOLOGY ALERT

Dangerous Foods Birds Must Never Consume

Birds possess rapid metabolisms and compact digestive tracts. Everyday human foods that seem harmless can trigger rapid respiratory collapse or fatal organ failure.

Avocado (Persin Toxin)

Persin toxin in avocado skin, flesh, and pit causes acute heart muscle necrosis, fluid around the heart, respiratory failure, and sudden death within 12–24 hours.

Toxic Alert Details →

Chocolate & Cocoa

Theobromine & caffeine cause severe vomiting, rapid hyperactivity, heart arrhythmias, tremors, seizures, and fatal nervous system breakdown.

Toxic Alert Details →

Caffeine (Coffee & Teas)

Caffeine alkaloids dramatically spike avian heart rates, inducing dangerous metabolic hyperactivity, cardiac arrhythmias, and sudden cardiac arrest.

Toxic Alert Details →

Alcohol & Spirits

Ethanol depressant mere drops cause hypothermia, severe loss of coordination, acute respiratory failure, irreversible liver damage, and death.

Toxic Alert Details →

Apple Seeds & Stone Pits

Cyanogenic glycosides in apple seeds, pear cores, and stone fruit pits break down into lethal cyanide when crushed by beaks; always core fruit.

Toxic Alert Details →

Onions & Garlic

Thiosulfates & disulfides in allium family plants rupture avian red blood cells, causing severe hemolytic anemia, crop ulcers, and gastric damage.

Toxic Alert Details →

High-Sodium Table Food

Excessive sodium ion load from chips, crackers, and salted snacks severely upsets avian electrolyte balances, causing kidney strain and dehydration.

Toxic Alert Details →

Rhubarb Stems & Leaves

Soluble oxalates bind essential circulating calcium in the bloodstream, forming insoluble crystals that damage renal tubules and cause acute kidney failure.

Toxic Alert Details →

Xylitol & Sweeteners

Synthetic sugar replacers in baked diet goods and gums cause severe metabolic distress, rapid hepatic necrosis, and life-threatening hypoglycemia.

VITAL HYDRATION

Fresh Water & Hydration Essentials

Clean water is indispensable for avian crop digestion, temperature regulation, and kidney wellness. Birds can fatally dehydrate in 24–48 hours.

Twice-Daily Water Changes

Birds frequently bathe or dunk pellets in their dishes; refresh drinking water every morning and late afternoon to ensure cleanliness.

Daily Routine →

Stainless Steel or Glass

Use non-porous stainless steel or heavy glazed ceramic bowls that resist microscopic scratches and stop bacterial biofilms from forming.

Sanitization Tips →

Daily Disinfection

Wash water dishes daily using hot water and bird-safe, unscented soap; rinse thoroughly until zero slippery residue remains.

Store-bought seed mixes are excessively high in saturated fats while lacking essential Vitamin A, Vitamin D3, calcium, and amino acids. An all-seed diet frequently leads to hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver disease), respiratory infections, brittle beaks, and drastically reduced life expectancies. Formulated pellets combined with fresh vegetables are essential for longevity.

Most vegetables can be served raw or gently steamed (without salt, butter, or oil). Dark leafy greens, broccoli, carrots, and bell peppers are excellent raw. Hard vegetables like sweet potatoes and squash are easier to digest when lightly steamed. Always ensure dried beans and legumes are thoroughly cooked, as raw dried beans contain hemagglutinin toxins.

Moist fresh produce, sprouted seeds, and cooked grains cultivate rapid bacterial proliferation (like Salmonella and E. coli) and fungal mold spores at room temperature. Removing uneaten fresh food within 1 to 2 hours (or up to 4 hours in cool indoor rooms) protects your bird from crop infections and severe enteritis.
